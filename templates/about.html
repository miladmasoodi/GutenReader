About GutenReader

Why?

Project Gutenberg

Project Gutenberg, named after Johannes Gutenberg, the inventor of the movable type printing press, began in 1971 as a visionary endeavor to digitize and make accessible the vast expanse of literature in the public domain. Johannes Gutenberg's invention in the mid-15th century revolutionized the production of books, making literature more accessible and affordable, and laid the foundation for the widespread dissemination of knowledge. Inspired by this transformative innovation, Michael S. Hart founded Project Gutenberg with a similar mission for the digital age. The project started with the digitization of the United States Declaration of Independence, and over the decades, it has grown exponentially, now offering tens of thousands of free eBooks. By leveraging the power of the internet, Project Gutenberg continues to fulfill its mission of democratizing access to literature, echoing Gutenberg's original impact on the availability of the written word.

Why?

Public domain books, especially classic literature, offer a wealth of benefits to readers and society. One of the key advantages is that these timeless works are freely accessible to everyone. This means anyone with an internet connection can enjoy the masterpieces of authors like Shakespeare, Dickens, and Austen without any cost. Such accessibility democratizes knowledge, allowing students, educators, and literature enthusiasts to explore these important texts without financial barriers.

Classic literature in the public domain also helps preserve our cultural heritage, ensuring that the stories and ideas that have shaped our history remain available for future generations. Moreover, these works provide endless opportunities for creativity and inspiration, as writers and artists can adapt and reinterpret them in new and exciting ways. In essence, public domain classics enrich our lives by making invaluable cultural and educational resources available to all.
